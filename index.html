Group Leader

Professor Atul Suresh Deshpande

Professor Deshpande is affiliated with IITH with extensive international research experience from the Max Planck Institute (Germany) and the University of Pittsburgh (USA). His work focuses on the synthesis and structural analysis of novel materials for energy and biomimetic applications.

Education

  • Ph.D. (2004): Max Planck Institute of Colloids and Interfaces, Germany
  • M.Sc. Inorganic Chemistry: University of Pune, India.
  • B.Sc. Chemistry: Sir Parashurambhau College, Pune

Overall Research Focus

Pioneering Solutions in Material Science

Our group operates at the intersection of Materials Engineering and Biomimetics. We are dedicated to understanding the fundamental mechanisms of synthesis to develop innovative technologies that address real-world challenges.

Our work is currently driven by three primary pillars:

  • Chemistry: Exploring the synthesis and properties of novel materials.
  • Materials Science: Understanding the structure-property relationships in advanced materials.
  • Engineering: Bridging the gap between theoretical frameworks and practical applications.

Core Research Areas

Carbon Nanoparticles

Carbon nanoparticles exhibit unique optical, electrical, and structural properties. Our research investigates their fundamental characteristics, focusing on their synthesis, functionalization, and behavior at the nanoscale.

  • Synthesis and characterization
  • Surface functionalization
  • Pore engineering

Designing Carbon Nanoparticles

Through advanced synthesis routes and novel approaches, we tailor the properties of carbon nanomaterials for specific applications. This involves predicting process-structure relationships and optimizing material performance through synthesis.

  • Rational material design
  • Synthesis optimization
  • Performance optimization

Research Focus

Nanoparticle Synthesis

Focusing on multicomponent oxides (high entropy oxides) and high entropy alloy nanoparticles, including detailed structural and functional analysis.

Carbon Materials

Generation of nanostructured carbon materials derived from biomass (like Okra and Corn husk) for energy storage applications.

Biomaterials

Biomimetic approaches for superhydrophobic surfaces, coatings, and bone regeneration scaffolds.
